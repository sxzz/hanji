# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

Readers, students, researchers, and language-tool builders who need to check
how a Han character is written and catalogued across mainland China, Hong
Kong, Taiwan, Japan, and Korea. Their core job is to determine regional forms,
listing status, and relationships quickly and reliably, whether they are
scanning the full table or looking up one character directly.

## Product Purpose

Hanji is a fast, transparent reference for the union of five regional common
character lists. It places regional glyphs, code points, stroke counts,
frequency, readings, listing status, related forms, and dictionary references
side by side. Success means a reader can verify a difference without mistaking
a typeface design choice, an unlisted reference glyph, or an uncertain
relationship for an official regional standard.

## Positioning

Hanji is a controlled cross-regional comparison rather than a dictionary or a
character-conversion table. It compares region-specific mappings inside the
shared Source Han Sans and Source Han Serif glyph pools, displays matching Noto
CJK regional forms, and conservatively keeps weakly supported cross-codepoint
relationships separate. The comparison method, source provenance, uncertainty,
and limitations remain visible to the reader.

## Operating Context

Readers can search and scan the full character table, filter by meaningful
difference patterns, sort by regional frequency, stroke count, or code point,
and choose the regions participating in a comparison. Character detail pages
provide stable direct URLs, regional metadata, readings, stroke-order material
where available, related forms, and links to external dictionaries. The
interface supports Simplified Chinese, Hong Kong Traditional Chinese, Taiwan
Traditional Chinese, Japanese, and Korean. The product is a static web app
with no account or server-side workflow, and its generated datasets are also
published as downloadable files.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- The product covers the union of the mainland Chinese, Hong Kong, Taiwan,
  Japanese, and Korean common-character lists. The current generated dataset
  contains 8,449 character groups.
- Each regional cell distinguishes primary entries, glossed variants, and
  unlisted reference forms. An unlisted form does not claim local adoption or
  official status.
- Glyph equivalence is derived from Source Han Sans and Source Han Serif CMap
  data. A difference that appears in only one of the two typeface families is
  treated as a typeface design detail rather than a regional norm.
- The comparison covers general-purpose printed sans and serif forms. It does
  not cover handwriting habits or claim to reproduce every regional teaching
  form.
- Cross-codepoint groups are merged only when the available regional lists and
  mappings provide sufficient evidence. Uncertain relationships remain
  display-only references and do not become search aliases or canonical URLs.
- Frequency rankings are available for mainland China, Hong Kong, Taiwan, and
  Japan. No Korean frequency corpus is currently included.
- Data, font subsets, redirects, and static character routes are generated by
  repository scripts. Generated outputs must be changed through their sources
  and build pipeline rather than edited by hand.
- Hanji is an independent reference built from public materials. It is not an
  official regional standard, dictionary, or teaching resource.

## Brand Commitments

The product name is “Hanji,” with the Chinese name “汉智.” “Hanji” sits one
letter away from _hanzi_, _kanji_, and _hanja_, and is also the Minnan reading
of 漢字. The voice is precise, neutral, and scholarly. Product language must not
rank regions, hide source limitations, or imply authority the project does not
have. Regional font selection must remain independent of the interface
language so that every comparison displays the intended local form.

## Evidence on Hand

- `README.md` and the localized About page document scope, methodology,
  limitations, naming, and deployment.
- `app/assets/data/chars.json` provides the generated character data through a
  Vite-fingerprinted asset. Static generation also copies it to the stable
  `/data/chars.json` URL for external consumers. Attribution metadata lives in
  `shared/sources.ts`, while `public/notices/data-sources.md` keeps
  transformation and license notes at a stable, revalidated URL.
- `data/sources.lock.json` pins third-party inputs and checksums.
- The data and font pipelines under `scripts/`, together with their Vitest
  suites, encode and verify grouping, source, mapping, and rendered-outline
  guarantees.
- Region-specific Noto CJK font subsets and local interface fonts are generated
  into `app/assets/fonts/`, with the applicable font license included in
  `public/notices/`.
- The repository contains no testimonials, institutional endorsements,
  commercial benchmarks, or other proof that future work may fabricate.

## Product Principles

- Put glyph comparison first and keep supporting metadata in its service.
- Make provenance, uncertainty, and scope explicit instead of smoothing over
  inconvenient distinctions.
- Preserve regional typographic fidelity independently of interface language.
- Keep direct lookup, filtering, and returning to context fast and predictable.
- Describe the project as an evidence-based reference without overstating its
  authority.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The project does not declare a formal WCAG conformance level. Existing product
requirements include visible keyboard focus, reduced-motion support, readable
light and dark themes, and comparison signals that remain understandable
without color. Locale selection and regional font handling must stay explicit
so readers receive the intended language and character form.
